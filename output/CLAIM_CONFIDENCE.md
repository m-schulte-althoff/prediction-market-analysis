# Confidence in the current research claims

Assessment: September 14, 2026. Evidence: the September 10 API snapshots and the corrected analysis, comprising 12,251 Kalshi and 7,249 Polymarket contracts, with the representation-aware formalization and complete-rule relation audit added September 14. No new empirical sample or activity model was added. This persistent assessment complements the generated reports.

**Overall assessment:** confidence is high that the archived contracts can define meaningfully different payable claims. Confidence is also high in the reported sample descriptions, conditional on the text measures used. Confidence is moderate in a generalizable determinacy–activity association and low in a causal effect of ambiguity, an accuracy improvement, or a realized price-distortion mechanism.

“High,” “moderate,” and “low” are reasoned evidence assessments, not numerical posterior probabilities. Statistical significance concerns sampling uncertainty under a model; it does not establish practical importance, construct validity, or causation.

## How to interpret “real and meaningful”

- **Mechanism existence:** a coherent scenario with different implied settlements establishes non-equivalence under the archived rules. It does not require a statistical test. The claim should concern the mapping specified by the rules, not automatically the platform's behavior in a disputed real settlement.
- **Economic importance:** a 0-versus-1 conditional payoff difference can be large for someone holding that position. Its average importance depends on the probability of the separating states, stakes, timing, and how much traders already price the distinction. Those quantities are not estimated. The primary-advancement example is especially useful because it does not depend on an unusual death or rate decision.
- **Observed association:** the inverse volume pattern has consistent support across several existing specifications, but a large sample and small p-value do not remove confounding. The preferred coefficient's HC3 uncertainty treats residual observations as independent; the separately reported clustered baseline is a different specification.
- **Measurement validity:** the text features are transparent and reproducible, not a validated scale of all institutional determinacy. Neither the case labels nor the scale have an independent human inter-coder reliability estimate in the current package. Passing code tests cannot substitute for that distinction.
- **Statistical interpretation:** intervals are reported 95% model intervals. Most volume outcomes and predictors are standardized within platform; within-family coefficients use internally varying families' residual-score SDs. P-values are the pipeline's exploratory, unadjusted values. No calibrated probability that a substantive theory is true is implied.

For the paper, foreground claims 1–9, use claim 10 as secondary empirical evidence, and keep claims 11–15 as interpretation boundaries or supplementary findings. Claims 16, 18 and 19 sharpen the theoretical mechanism; claim 17 is a design implication and claim 20 an explicit evidence boundary. The most defensible central contribution is that prediction platforms specify payoff-relevant claims over histories before markets aggregate valuations of those claims. Identical posterior world beliefs can therefore yield different claim probabilities, while an incomplete institutional mapping cannot simply be assumed to supply a unique claim event.
