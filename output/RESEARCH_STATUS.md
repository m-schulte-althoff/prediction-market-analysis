# Research Status

Updated 2026-09-11.

## Current core

The strongest contribution is an audited account of how resolution architectures turn one public-world phenomenon into non-equivalent digital claims. The case matrix currently contains 5 mechanisms with exact rules and counterfactual witness states.

## Current empirical package

The corrected non-sports metadata sample contains 19,500 contracts.
- Preferred cohort/exposure-adjusted determinacy–volume estimate: -0.115 (HC3 SE 0.009, p=2.16e-35, n=19,500).
- Within-family Kalshi estimate: -0.035 (clustered SE 0.066, p=0.597, n=6,443).
- Within-family Polymarket estimate: 0.031 (clustered SE 0.027, p=0.262, n=1,043).
- Contract-type split: qualitative -0.023 (SE 0.018, p=0.198, n=7,725); quantitative -0.049 (SE 0.014, p=0.00048, n=11,775).
- Temporal/platform split: early kalshi -0.179; early polymarket -0.021; late kalshi -0.068; late polymarket -0.134.

These volume results are exploratory consequences, not the theory’s sole support. The tiny aligned-price panel does not identify a divergence effect.

## Locked decisions

- Keep determinacy (within contract) separate from divergence (between contracts).
- Require a witness state before labeling truth-condition divergence.
- Keep automated text-distance scores as candidate diagnostics until validated coding exists.
- Exclude sports before Polymarket’s high-volume cap and control cumulative-volume exposure/cohort.
